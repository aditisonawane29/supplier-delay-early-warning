How the risk is calculated

  • For each supplier, deliveries are sorted by planned date and we use the latest 10 (or all if there are fewer).
  • A delivery is late when its actual date is after its planned date. Days late is the number of calendar days after the plan; early or on-time deliveries count as 0.
  • Late rate is late deliveries divided by deliveries counted. Average days late uses only late deliveries.
  • Trend compares the latest five deliveries with the five before them: a higher late rate is “Getting worse,” a lower rate is “Improving,” and an equal rate (or too little history to compare) is “Stable.”
  • Risk is “On track” below 10%, “Watch” from 10% through 30%, and “High risk” above 30% or from 20% through 30% when the trend is getting worse.

The overall on-time rate uses all valid deliveries in your file. Supplier risk uses each supplier’s latest 10 deliveries.
